## Check Your Air Filter First

Arizona's desert environment is hard on air filters. Dust storms, construction debris, and everyday particulate matter clog filters faster than in most parts of the country. A dirty filter restricts airflow across the evaporator coil, which can cause the coil to freeze — and a frozen coil means no cooling.

Pull your filter out and hold it up to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it immediately. During peak cooling season in the Phoenix Valley, checking your filter monthly isn't overkill — it's necessary.

> **Key Takeaway:** A clogged air filter is the single most common reason an AC runs without cooling. In Arizona's dusty climate, check yours every 30 days — not every 90 like the packaging suggests.

## Understand the 20-Degree Rule

Here's something many Phoenix homeowners don't realize: your [air conditioning system](https://www.viking-hvac.com/services/air-conditioning) is designed to cool your home approximately 20 degrees below the outdoor temperature. When Phoenix hits 115°F, your AC working at full capacity might only achieve 95°F indoors.

That's not a malfunction — that's your system working as designed. If your home is holding at roughly 20 degrees below the outside temperature, your AC is performing correctly even though it feels warm inside.

Where it becomes a problem is when the gap is much larger than 20 degrees — if it's 110°F outside and 100°F inside, something is wrong and you should call a licensed technician.

> **Key Takeaway:** Your AC isn't broken just because your house feels warm on the hottest days. The 20-degree differential is normal. If the gap is wider than that, it's time to call for service.

## Low Refrigerant Is the Second Most Common Culprit

If your filter is clean and your system still isn't cooling, low refrigerant is likely. Refrigerant doesn't get "used up" — if it's low, you have a leak somewhere. This is especially common in older systems where connections and coils develop small cracks from years of thermal cycling in Arizona's extreme temperature swings.

Signs of low refrigerant include ice forming on the refrigerant lines outside, a hissing or bubbling sound near the indoor unit, and your system running constantly without reaching the thermostat's set temperature.

This isn't a DIY fix. Refrigerant handling requires [EPA certification](https://www.epa.gov/section608), and finding the leak matters more than just topping off the charge.
